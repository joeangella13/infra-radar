### FERC denies ArcLight-backed developer's waiver to modify a $2B PJM fast-track gas plant, enforcing RRI rigidity

`2026-07-02` · Tracked situations · [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-pjm-fast-track-review-advanced-power-chestnut-run/824456/)

**Deal / players.** FERC on July 2, 2026 denied a waiver sought by Advanced Power Services, owned by ArcLight Capital Partners, to reduce the maximum output of its roughly $2 billion Chestnut Run gas plant in Carroll County, Ohio from 1.3 GW to 1.245 GW after equipment supply constraints forced a turbine substitution. The project entered PJM's Reliability Resource Initiative, an expedited process that initially covered 51 projects totalling roughly 9.3-11.8 GW and prohibits changes to project size and capacity rights. FERC found studies from the equipment change would introduce substantial delays to Transition Cycle #2 with ripple effects across PJM's study schedule.

**Why it matters.** FERC denied a waiver sought by ArcLight-owned Advanced Power Services to cut its roughly $2bn Chestnut Run gas plant in Ohio from 1.3 GW to 1.245 GW after supply constraints forced a turbine substitution. PJM's Reliability Resource Initiative bars changes to project size, and FERC found the restudy would delay Transition Cycle #2. Fast-track interconnection now clearly carries zero flexibility, raising the value of secured turbine slots.

**Why you care.** Sits on your ArcLight/Avangrid coverage and makes a sharp interview point: an RRI queue position is only as good as the equipment order already locked behind it.

### DigitalBridge to acquire power investor ArcLight Capital Partners for about $1.05bn

`2026-05-27` · Tracked situations · [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/digitalbridge-arclight-data-center-deal/821199/), [Infrastructure Investor](https://www.infrastructureinvestor.com/this-just-makes-sense-why-digitalbridge-and-arclight-are-joining-forces/)

**Deal / players.** DigitalBridge agreed to acquire ArcLight Capital Partners, the private power investment manager, for approximately $1.05bn, creating a combined manager spanning power, AI and digital infrastructure. ArcLight brought roughly 20.8GW of owned generation capacity (as of June 2025), about 7GW of which sits in PJM, plus a circa 15GW development pipeline. ArcLight will run as a separately managed business; the deal is contingent on SoftBank Group completing its separate $4bn acquisition of DigitalBridge.

**Why it matters.** The clearest GP-level bet that data centre returns are gated by electrons, not racks: DigitalBridge is buying dispatchable generation, 20.8GW owned with about 7GW in PJM plus a 15GW pipeline, instead of more colocation, so it can sell power-plus-shell to hyperscalers. At roughly $1.05bn this is a management-company price, meaning GP consolidation is now driven by capability gaps. Contingent on SoftBank's separate $4bn DigitalBridge deal.

**Why you care.** You have worked opposite ArcLight. This changes who that counterparty becomes and gives you a sharp answer on why power is the AI bottleneck.
