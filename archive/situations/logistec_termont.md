**Deal / players.** LOGISTEC deployed two 120-tonne harbor cranes at Care Terminal at the Port of Houston, operated by Gulf Stream Marine within the LOGISTEC network, describing it as the first phase of a broader multi-stage investment program covering berthing, rail, yard upgrades, expanded storage and improved access. The terminal handles breakbulk and project cargo. CEO Sean Pierce said the cranes strengthen LOGISTEC's long-term Gulf Coast strategy. **Why it matters.** Two 120-tonne cranes give the Houston breakbulk terminal the heavy-lift capability that wins wind components, modular process equipment and oversized project cargo, the highest-rate Gulf categories. Calling it phase one of a multi-stage program shows Blue Wolf funding organic capacity alongside M&A. Capex is undisclosed, so returns can't be run; the phasing suggests staged approval against volumes.

**Why you care.** Blue Wolf funding organic capacity on the platform you ran at Rothschild; heavy-lift capability wins the highest-rate Gulf cargo, though undisclosed capex means the phase-one return cannot be run.

**Deal / players.** LOGISTEC announced the acquisition of Logistics Park Dubuque, a 100-acre multimodal terminal in East Dubuque, Illinois on the Upper Mississippi River handling bulk and breakbulk commodities via barge, CN rail and truck, from Travero, a subsidiary of Alliant Energy Corporation. Price was not disclosed. Berenson & Company advised LOGISTEC financially with King & Spalding as legal counsel; RJM & Company advised Travero with Chapman & Cutler as counsel. **Why it matters.** East Dubuque gives LOGISTEC CN rail plus Upper Mississippi barge access into Midwest agriculture, steel and energy flows, tying its Gulf Coast and Great Lakes positions together. Buying from a utility parent shedding non-core logistics is a repeatable sourcing channel, and Alliant's willingness to sell suggests more utility-owned terminals could come to market. Undisclosed pricing here and on Altamira means no multiple reference for the roll-up.

### LOGISTEC signs definitive agreement to acquire IPA breakbulk and steel terminal at Port of Altamira, Mexico

`2026-02-17` · Tracked situations · [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/logistec-expands-international-reach-through-acquisition-agreement-of-ipa-terminal-in-altamira-mexico-302689698.html)

**Deal / players.** LOGISTEC entered a definitive agreement to acquire the IPA Terminal at the Port of Altamira, Mexico, a specialised breakbulk and steel handling facility operating under the IPA, ATEMSA, SMA and STEEL entities with deepwater access. Price was not disclosed. Completion remains subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals from Mexico's Comisión Nacional Antimonopolio and the Mexican Port Authorities and Navy, with no timeline provided. Berenson & Company advised on financial matters and Holland & Knight on legal. CEO Sean Pierce framed the deal as accelerated international growth extending LOGISTEC's network to Latin America.

**Why it matters.** LOGISTEC's first Latin American move: a steel and breakbulk terminal at Altamira that pairs with Houston into a two-country steel corridor rather than two standalone terminals. Signed but not closed, with Mexican antitrust plus port authority and Navy clearance outstanding and no timetable, so the approval path is less predictable than a US filing. With Dubuque and the Houston cranes, Blue Wolf is building a Gulf-plus-inland network.

**Why you care.** You ran LOGISTEC/Termont at Rothschild, and the platform is buying into Mexico - signed, not closed, pending antitrust, port and Navy clearance. A live sourcing angle worth flagging at MSIP.
