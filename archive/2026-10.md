### FERC accepts Duke's 75%, 15-year minimum-payment floor for large-load transmission customers

`2026-10-09` · Market / regulatory · [mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/2026/10/10/ferc-accepts-dukes-75-15-year-floor-on-large-load-transmission-costs-and-a-commissioner-calls-it-the-low-end/)

**Deal / players.** In docket ER26-2691, FERC accepted terms Duke wrote into its standard network transmission service agreement. A customer serving a large load must pay for at least 75% of its planned load for 15 years, even if actual load comes in lower. The order also makes rolled-in pricing the default for network upgrades that large loads drive. Commissioner Lindsay See concurred on Oct 9 and urged Duke to use the higher incremental rate where policy allows. She said the order does not prejudge the June 2026 large-load show-cause cases.

**Why it matters.** A 75% take-or-pay for 15 years is a concrete, FERC-approved floor on large-load demand. It cuts stranded-cost risk for utilities and for generation built to serve data centres. Rolled-in upgrade pricing still spreads some cost to other customers, and a commissioner called the floor the low end. Expect pressure for tighter terms. Watch the Oct 15 open meeting and the Oct 21 reliability conference.

**Why you care.** A 75%, 15-year payment floor is a live reference point when you test load and take-or-pay assumptions on powered-land, data-centre or utility-linked deals at MSIP.

### FERC rejects PJM's curtail-first plan for new large loads; keeps only 50 MW definition and a load registry

`2026-10-09` · Market / regulatory · [mgrid.org](https://mgrid.org/2026/10/10/ferc-rejects-pjms-curtail-first-plan-for-new-large-loads-and-keeps-only-the-50-mw-definition-and-a-registry/)

**Deal / players.** FERC ruled on Oct 9 in docket ER26-3515-000. It rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service, which would have let a new data centre connect before matching capacity existed, with PJM able to curtail it first. It also rejected the 'bring your own new capacity' rules. It accepted only the 50 MW large-load definition and a registry of each large load's location, ramp schedule and capacity supply. PJM had filed Aug 13 and asked for an Oct 12 effective date.

**Why it matters.** FERC's objection was jurisdiction, not the idea: it said the proposal put obligations on retail customers and chose who gets curtailed, which belongs to states. That leaves PJM with no fast-connect route for new data centres. Self-supply and co-located power deals look more valuable as a result. Watch PJM's next filing and which states act on curtailment.

**Why you care.** A clear pricing and risk input for any PJM generation or data-centre power case you underwrite: the quick grid-connection route for large loads is gone for now.
