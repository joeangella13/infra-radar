### Bain Capital explores selling up to 50% of Singapore's Bridge DC at a roughly $4B valuation

`2026-07-29` · Sector themes · [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/sixth-street-gic-sk-telecom-la-caisse-compete-to-buy-stake-in-bridge-dc-report/)

**Deal / players.** Bain Capital is reported to be exploring a sale of up to a 50% stake in Singapore-based Bridge Data Centres at approximately a $4 billion valuation, with Sixth Street Partners, SK Telecom, GIC and La Caisse among the bidders. Bridge has five data centers operational or planned in Malaysia, two in Thailand and one in India, and targets 3 GW of capacity by 2030. Founded in 2017, Bridge was merged with Bain's ChinData business in 2019 and separated after ChinData's sale in September 2025. Bridge declined to comment on what it called unverified market information; no final decisions have been made.

**Why it matters.** Bain is reported to be exploring a sale of up to 50% of Bridge Data Centres at roughly $4bn, with Sixth Street, SK Telecom, GIC and La Caisse among bidders; nothing has been decided. That is a useful pricing reference for emerging-market data centre growth capital, valued on pipeline (Bridge targets 3GW by 2030) rather than stabilised NOI, and it confirms Malaysia and Thailand as Singapore's overflow markets.

**Why you care.** A live process to track. Asian DC platform pricing, and the bidder list shows which capital pools now compete for digital infrastructure.

### Temasek, GIC and CalPERS revealed as co-investors in Macquarie-led A$11.7bn Qube take-private

`2026-05-14` · Sponsor moves · [Infrastructure Investor](https://www.infrastructureinvestor.com/temasek-gic-calpers-revealed-as-investors-in-macquarie-led-bid-to-acquire-qube/), [MSN / AFR](https://www.msn.com/en-au/money/economy/supreme-court-approves-11-7b-macquarie-asset-management-led-takeover-of-qube/ar-AA27qPOG)

**Deal / players.** Infrastructure Investor identified Temasek, GIC and CalPERS as co-investors in the Macquarie Asset Management-led consortium bidding for Australian ports and logistics operator Qube Holdings. The scheme was agreed in February 2026 at about A$11.7bn (roughly US$8.3bn) enterprise value, A$5.20 per share in cash, via MAM's MAIF 4 vehicle. The New South Wales Supreme Court subsequently approved the scheme in July 2026.

**Why it matters.** The largest Australian infrastructure take-private of the cycle, at roughly A$11.7bn enterprise value and A$5.20 a share in cash, shows listed logistics platforms with landside port concessions still clear at a premium when sovereign and pension money syndicates behind a manager. That co-investment is what allows a cheque this size without a GP club. The NSW court approved the scheme in July.

**Why you care.** Benchmark for how a large ports-and-logistics platform gets funded, and a concrete example of co-invest structure if fund strategy comes up.
