### Infralogic: Q3 infrastructure fundraising led by KKR $19.2bn, Partners Group $15bn; nine-month total 39% below 2025

`2026-10-13` · Market / regulatory · [Infralogic (ION Analytics) Global Fundraising Report 3Q26](https://ionanalytics.com/insights/infralogic/global-fundraising-report-3q26-mega-fund-closes-driving-volumes/)

**Deal / players.** Infralogic's 3Q26 report (Oct 13) says KKR Global Infrastructure Investors V led with a $19.2bn close, then Partners Group Direct Infrastructure IV at $15bn and Energy Capital Partners VI at $8.1bn. The nine-month raise is $97.2bn against $159.5bn a year earlier. Infrastructure funds raised $51.3bn in the quarter, flat on 3Q25's $51.4bn, and 17 funds launched, down from 24 in 2Q26.

**Why it matters.** Three large closes drove the quarter, while nine-month volume sits 39% below 2025's record. Capital is pooling at the biggest managers, which points to well-funded bidders for large assets and slower going for smaller sponsors. Watch the 17 new launches, down from 24 in 2Q, for how much of the mid-market fund pipeline thins out.

**Why you care.** Background for how MSIP's own fundraising and co-invest conversations sit in a market where LPs are favouring the biggest managers.

### Norway's NBIM commits €1.2bn to Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners' new flagship renewables fund, CI VI

`2026-10-05` · Market / regulatory · [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/nbim-commits-over-1-3-billion-to-cips-new-flagship-clean-energy-infrastructure-fund/)

**Deal / players.** Norges Bank Investment Management committed €1.2bn (about $1.34bn) to CIP's CI VI fund, which invests mainly in renewable generation and storage across OECD markets. CIP is reportedly seeking €16bn for the fund; its predecessor, CIP V, closed in 2025 at €12bn. NBIM put €900m into CIP V in 2024.

**Why it matters.** A single anchor ticket of this size shows big sovereign LPs are still writing large cheques for development-stage renewables despite the sector's wobbles. A €16bn reported target would be a third larger than CIP V, which means more dry powder bidding for wind, solar and storage platforms. Watch for the first close and how CIP prices its next deals.

**Why you care.** CIP is a watchlist rival for renewable and storage platforms, so its fundraising pace is a read on competition and pricing for deals MSIP might look at.

### Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners targets €16bn for sixth flagship renewables fund

`2026-06-19` · Sponsor moves · [Infrastructure Investor](https://www.infrastructureinvestor.com/cip-eyes-e16bn-for-latest-renewables-flagship-exclusive/)

**Deal / players.** Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners is targeting €16bn for CI VI, its sixth flagship renewables vehicle. The predecessor, CI V, closed above its €12bn target in March 2025. First close timing and terms were not disclosed.

**Why it matters.** A EUR 16bn target is a one-third step-up on a predecessor that closed above its EUR 12bn target, and it tests whether greenfield renewables can still absorb mega-fund capital as US tax credits sunset and European auction pricing resets. CIP creates value development-to-COD, so returns hinge on locking grid connections and offtake before construction capital is committed. The read-through is that specialist greenfield platforms remain fundable at scale while generalist energy transition funds struggle.

**Why you care.** LP capital still backs specialist greenfield platforms at scale, a EUR 16bn target against a predecessor that beat EUR 12bn. Fundraising backdrop rather than anything actionable.
