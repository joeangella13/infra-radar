### Grain Management combines Ritter and Great Plains Communications into 20-state, 28,000-mile fiber platform Rightfiber

`2026-06-16` · Sponsor moves · [Grain Management](https://graingp.com/ritter-communications-and-great-plains-communications-combine-to-form-rightfiber/), [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/grain-management-to-combine-ritter-great-plains-into-20-state-fiber-platform/)

**Deal / players.** Grain Management combined portfolio companies Ritter Communications (Jonesboro, Arkansas) and Great Plains Communications (Blair, Nebraska) under a new unified brand, Rightfiber. Terms undisclosed. The combined business operates a 28,000-mile regional fiber network across 20 states, serving over 400 communities and 300,000 connected homes and businesses. Heath Simpson, Ritter CEO, leads Rightfiber; Todd Foje, Great Plains CEO, becomes executive chairman. The company will pursue growth through both network expansion and M&A.

**Why it matters.** Merging Ritter and Great Plains into Rightfiber creates a 28,000-mile network across 20 states, crossing the scale where hyperscaler and enterprise wholesale demand becomes addressable; neither company could bid long-haul or middle-mile alone. The mix improves too, since enterprise and carrier transport on already-built route is high-margin, unlike capital-hungry residential FTTH. One combined asset also sets up a single cleaner exit.

**Why you care.** Buy-and-build logic in regional fibre: 28,000 route miles crosses into hyperscaler and enterprise wholesale demand neither company could bid alone. Market read on fibre scale, not a live angle.

The real prize is four data centres plus the Detroit Internet Exchange carrier hotel, interconnection revenue with utility-like margins and low churn.

### Grain-backed Great Plains Communications to acquire Fastwyre Broadband's Nebraska operations across 26+ communities

`2026-05-19` · Sponsor moves · [Grain Management](https://graingp.com/great-plains-communications-to-acquire-fastwyre-broadband-in-nebraska/)

**Deal / players.** Great Plains Communications (GPC), a Grain Management portfolio company, agreed to acquire the Nebraska broadband business of American Broadband Holding Company dba Fastwyre Broadband. Terms undisclosed. The acquisition covers residential and business customers in 26+ Nebraska communities including Bellevue, Blair, Fremont, Wayne, Tekamah and Winnebago, and brings over two dozen Nebraska-based Fastwyre employees to GPC. Close is subject to customary conditions and regulatory approvals. GPC operates a MEF-certified 20,000+ mile fiber network. CEO Todd Foje called it a strategic milestone in expanding the Midwest fiber network.

**Why it matters.** Grain-backed Great Plains Communications agreed to acquire Fastwyre's Nebraska broadband business, covering 26+ communities and over two dozen staff, terms undisclosed and subject to regulatory approval. In-footprint consolidation is the cheapest form of fibre M&A because subscribers migrate onto GPC's existing 20,000-mile network with minimal new capex. It landed weeks before GPC was folded into Rightfiber alongside Ritter, so Grain was buying density ahead of the platform combination.

**Why you care.** Grain bought in-footprint density weeks before folding GPC into Rightfiber, the cheapest form of fibre M&A executed immediately ahead of a platform combination. Market read on sequencing.

### Grain Management reinvests as Spectrotel combines with AireSpring; Charlesbank invests in managed network services platform

`2026-05-12` · Sponsor moves · [Grain Management](https://graingp.com/grain-management-announces-spectrotel-combination-with-airespring/)

**Deal / players.** Spectrotel Holding, LLC combined with AireSpring to create a managed network services platform, with Charlesbank Capital Partners making a new strategic investment and Grain Management reinvesting to support growth. Financial terms were not disclosed. Close was expected later in 2026. Moelis & Company advised Spectrotel, Charlesbank and Grain; Bank Street Group advised AireSpring. Legal advisers were Gibson Dunn (Spectrotel/Grain), Latham & Watkins and Wilkinson Barker Knauer (Charlesbank), and Alston & Bird (AireSpring).

**Why it matters.** Managed network services is asset-light contracted recurring revenue, so it trades on retention and gross margin rather than route miles, and merging two aggregators mainly buys carrier purchasing leverage. The interesting part is Grain reinvesting rather than exiting: partial liquidity, a reset hold clock and Charlesbank's capital for the next phase, which is what sponsors do when the consolidation runway outlasts the fund.

**Why you care.** Grain reinvesting rather than exiting is what sponsors do when the consolidation runway outlasts the fund, a hold-structure read from the wider sponsor set. Terms undisclosed.
