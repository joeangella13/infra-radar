### Blackstone, Brookfield and KKR sign $16bn Kuwait crude pipeline partnership, the country's largest ever FDI

`2026-07-28` · Sponsor moves · [Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/kuwait-oil-company-signs-us-16-0-billion-infrastructure-partnership-involving-its-crude-oil-pipeline-network-with-a-consortium-comprising-blackstone-brookfield-and-kkr/), [Rigzone](https://www.rigzone.com/news/kuwait_agrees_16b_pipeline_leaseback_with_blackstone_brookfield_kkr-27-jul-2026-184217-article/)

**Deal / players.** Kuwait Oil Company signed a $16bn infrastructure partnership, codenamed Project Peregrine, with a consortium of Blackstone, Brookfield and KKR covering 13 crude oil pipelines spanning roughly 320km of Kuwait's domestic and export network. The consortium takes 49% of the joint venture in equal roughly 16.33% stakes, with KOC retaining 51% and operational control; KOC receives $7.85bn of upfront proceeds at closing. The JV grants KOC exclusive use and operating rights for 20.5 years in exchange for a volume-based tariff; KPC called it the largest foreign direct investment in Kuwait's history.

**Why it matters.** In substance a sale-and-leaseback: the sponsors own pipelines they do not operate and collect a volume-based tariff from a state oil company for 20.5 years, so the risk is throughput and convertibility rather than operations. A volume-linked rather than fixed tariff gives growth participation but removes the availability-payment floor infrastructure buyers usually insist on. KOC keeps 51% and control, taking $7.85bn upfront.

**Why you care.** Three sponsors splitting 49% equally is how country risk gets spread, on a structure that is really a sale-and-leaseback with a volume tariff and no availability floor.

### Blackstone, Apollo and KKR to invest $5.34bn for 49% of Williams' behind-the-meter Power Innovation JV

`2026-07-13` · Sponsor moves · [Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/williams-announces-5-34-billion-investment-in-power-innovation-joint-venture-from-blackstone/)

**Deal / players.** Williams agreed a $5.34bn investment from Blackstone and partners Apollo and KKR for a 49% non-controlling equity interest in its Power Innovation joint venture, with Williams retaining 51% and operational control. The commitment comprises $4.4bn representing 49% of growth capex plus $0.9bn of additional consideration. The JV holds five behind-the-meter projects, Socrates, Apollo, Aquila, Socrates the Younger and Neo, part of Williams' 6GW-plus backlog; Williams holds buyout rights in years seven to fourteen at Blackstone's outstanding investment balance.

**Why it matters.** Structured equity dressed as a JV: Williams keeps 51% and control, and its buyout right in years seven to fourteen at Blackstone's outstanding investment balance caps sponsor upside, which is why three big managers could share the $5.34bn without competing on multiple. Behind-the-meter generation contracted to data centre load is the strongest cash-flow profile in US power now: long tenor, investment-grade counterparties, no merchant risk.

**Why you care.** The structure to know cold: minority structured equity into midstream-sponsored power, where Williams' year seven to fourteen buyout right caps sponsor upside, which is why three managers shared $5.34bn without bidding up multiple.

### KKR to acquire EDF power solutions' North American renewables business for $4.2bn

`2026-06-30` · Sponsor moves · [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260630667572/en/KKR-to-Acquire-EDF-power-solutions-North-American-Operations-for-$4.2-Billion), [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/kkr-acquires-edfs-north-american-renewable-energy-business-for-4-2-billion/)

**Deal / players.** KKR agreed to acquire EDF power solutions' US and Canadian operations for $4.2bn in equity consideration plus up to a further $0.39bn in contingent payments, buying through its Global Infrastructure Strategy. The business is among the top ten owners of US renewable capacity, with a diversified solar, wind and battery storage portfolio plus development, construction and O&M capability and a near 40-year operating history. Existing management continues; close is subject to regulatory approvals.

**Why it matters.** A $4.2bn carve-out from a seller under balance-sheet pressure is usually the best entry basis available in a crowded asset class. The point is buying development, construction and O&M alongside the operating fleet: that turns a portfolio purchase into a platform which originates at cost instead of competing at auction. The up-to-$390m contingent payment bridges a gap on pipeline value, sensible with US tax credits sunsetting. Close is subject to regulatory approvals.

**Why you care.** The comp for a large renewables platform trade, and it reads against your ArcLight/Avangrid work: the agreed $4.2bn carve-out buys origination capability, not just an operating fleet.
